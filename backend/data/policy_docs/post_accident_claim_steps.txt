Post Accident Steps and Claim Filing Guide

What to Do After the Immediate Accident Response

After ensuring safety, calling the police and ambulance if needed, and securing the scene, you must take the following steps to protect your insurance claim.

Within 24 Hours of the Accident

Notify Your Insurance Company
Call your insurance company within 24 hours of the accident. This is a policy requirement. Failure to report within this window may result in your claim being reduced or rejected. Have the following information ready when you call: your policy number, date and time of accident, location of accident, description of what happened, police report number, details of the other party involved, names and contact details of any witnesses.

File the Police Report
If you have not already done so at the scene, go to the nearest police station and file an official accident report. Ask for the police report number and a copy of the report. Keep this document safe as it is required for your claim.

Seek Medical Attention
Even if you feel fine immediately after the accident, see a doctor within 24 hours. Some injuries such as whiplash, concussion, and internal injuries may not show symptoms immediately. Medical records will support any personal injury component of your claim.

Do Not Repair the Vehicle Yet
Do not take your vehicle to a garage for repairs until the insurance company has assessed the damage. Unauthorized repairs before assessment may mean the insurance company will not cover the cost.

Within 7 Days of the Accident

Submit Your Claim Documents
Submit the following documents to your insurance company within 7 days of the accident: completed claim form, copy of police report, photographs of all vehicle damage, photographs of the accident scene, copy of your driving licence, copy of vehicle registration certificate, copy of your insurance certificate, repair estimates from authorized garages if requested, witness statements if available, medical reports if there are injuries.

Vehicle Inspection
The insurance company will arrange for an assessor to inspect your vehicle. Make your vehicle available for inspection promptly. The assessor will document the damage and estimate the repair cost.

Claim Assessment Process

Once your claim is submitted, the insurance company will review all documents, assess the vehicle damage, determine liability and coverage, calculate the claim amount, and notify you of the decision.

If Your Claim is Approved
You will receive written approval. The insurance company will either authorize repairs at an approved garage or offer a cash settlement. If repairs are authorized, take your vehicle to the approved garage and the insurance company will pay the garage directly minus your excess.

If Your Claim is Partially Approved or Rejected
You will receive a written explanation. You have the right to appeal the decision. You can also escalate to the Insurance Regulatory Commission of Sri Lanka if you believe the rejection is unfair.

Your Excess or Deductible

Every claim requires you to pay an excess also called a deductible. This is the amount you pay first before the insurance company pays the rest. For example if your repair costs are 150,000 rupees and your excess is 25,000 rupees, the insurance company pays 125,000 rupees and you pay 25,000 rupees. Check your policy document for your exact excess amount.

Recommended Garages

For best results and to avoid disputes, use an insurance company approved garage for repairs. Approved garages have agreed pricing with the insurer and their work is guaranteed. Using a non-approved garage may require you to pay upfront and claim reimbursement later, which takes longer.

Dealing with the Other Party

If the other party was at fault, their insurance company should cover your vehicle repair costs. However you may still need to file a claim with your own insurer who will then recover the costs from the at fault party's insurer. This process is called subrogation.

If you were at fault, your third party coverage will pay for the other party's damages and injuries. Your own vehicle damage is covered only if you have comprehensive coverage.

Legal Assistance

If the other party makes a legal claim against you, notify your insurance company immediately. Do not respond to any legal correspondence without consulting your insurer first. Your comprehensive policy includes third party liability coverage which pays for legal defense costs and any compensation awarded against you.
